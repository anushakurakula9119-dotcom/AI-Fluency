##Lesson 5: Capabilities & Limitations

Course: AI Fluency: Framework & Foundations (Claude Academy) Section: Deep Dive 1: What is Generative AI? (Part 1) Lesson length: 9 min (video 7 min)

**Learning goal**

Identify the major capabilities and limitations of current generative AI.

Note: the lesson lists these points in a short summary. The analogies and examples below are my own, in simple words.

1. **Capabilities (what AI is good at)**
Capability                                           	Simple meaning	                                                  Example
**Versatility across language tasks**                	Can do many kinds of text work	                                  Write, summarize, translate, explain, brainstorm
**Conversational flow**	                       Remembers what was said earlier in the chat and keeps the thread   	   Follow-up questions work without repeating everything
**Switching tasks without extra training**	   Can go from one job to a totally different one instantly	     Write a poem, then debug code, then plan a trip
**Connecting with external tools**	          Can use other tools (search, files, apps)	                       Look something up on the web, then use the result

2. **Limitations (where AI falls short)**
Limitation	                                 Simple meaning	                                                                           Analogy
**Knowledge cutoff date**          	Its knowledge stops at a point in time; it doesn't know later events unless it can search	               A textbook printed last year: new events aren't in it
**Hallucinations**	                          States things that sound right but are false	                                                 A confident student who guesses and sounds sure
**Context window limits**	                     Can only hold so much text in view at once	                                                   A desk that can only fit so many notebooks
**Reasoning challenges**	                   Can struggle with complex, multi-step reasoning	                                               Fine on easy sums, may slip on a long tricky puzzle

3. **The big lesson:** humans + AI together
The field is changing fast, so today's limits may shrink.

The most effective use combines human and AI strengths.

Humans bring: **critical thinking, judgment, creativity, ethical oversight.**
AI brings: **speed, versatility, handling lots of text.**

5. **Key takeaways (from the lesson)**
Generative AI creates new content (text, images, code), not only analyzes data.

**LLMs became possible through:** transformer architecture + vast training data + big computing power.

**Two learning stages:** **pre-training** (finding patterns across billions of examples) and **fine-tuning** (following instructions, being helpful).

**Capabilities:** versatility, conversational awareness, connecting to external tools.
**Limitations:** knowledge cutoff, hallucinations, context window limits, complex reasoning.
Best results come from combining human and AI strengths.

**Quick revision**
**Can do:** many language tasks, keep a conversation going, switch tasks, use tools.
**Can't always:** know recent events, avoid making things up, remember unlimited text, reason perfectly.

**Best approach:** AI + human judgment together.

**Link to AI Security (my goal)**
Hallucinations lead to false output that people may trust. This is a security and reliability risk.
Knowledge cutoff means the AI can give outdated security advice.
Context window is where hidden instructions (prompt injection) can be placed.
Tool connections raise the stakes: an AI that can act (send emails, run code) can cause real damage if tricked.
Human oversight is the main safeguard.
