Learning goals
Define generative AI and explain how it differs from other types of AI.
Recognize the key characteristics and technological foundations of generative AI.
1. What is generative AI?
Generative AI creates new content (text, images, music, code).
Older AI mostly analyzes existing content (labels, sorts, detects).
Older AI	Generative AI
Sorts and labels things	Makes new things
"Is this email spam?"	"Write me an email."
Like a teacher grading homework	Like a student writing the homework
2. How an LLM works
LLM = Large Language Model
Large: trained on a huge amount of text
Language: works with words
Model: a program that has learned patterns
Core idea: it predicts the next word based on the words so far, then repeats, one word at a time.
It does not look answers up in a book. It builds replies from learned patterns.

Example: "I brushed my teeth with a ___" leads to "toothbrush" or "toothpaste", because the pattern appears very often in what it learned.

3. What made LLMs possible (three ingredients)
Ingredient	Analogy	Meaning
Algorithmic breakthroughs (the transformer architecture)	The recipe	Lets the model pay attention to all words at once and see which matter to each other
Vast training datasets	The ingredients	Books, websites, articles, code: more reading means better patterns
Powerful computing	The oven	Fast chips that can process that much data

Transformer example: in "The dog chased the cat because it was hungry," the transformer helps figure out what "it" refers to.

4. How it learns (two stages)
Stage	What happens	Analogy
Pre-training	Reads huge amounts of text, practicing next-word prediction billions of times	A child who reads thousands of books and learns language
Fine-tuning	Further trained with examples and feedback to be helpful, safe, and honest	Finishing school: learning to answer politely and accurately
5. Related concepts
Context window: how much text the model can see and use at one time (its "desk").
Emergent capabilities: abilities nobody specifically taught that appear once models get big enough (e.g. translation, solving puzzles, writing code).
Quick revision
Generative AI creates; older AI mostly analyzes.
LLM = Large Language Model, which predicts the next word using learned patterns.
Ingredients: transformer + huge data + powerful computers.
Learning: pre-training (learn language) then fine-tuning (be helpful and safe).
Context window = what it can hold in view; emergent capabilities = surprise skills at scale.

Link to AI Security (my goal)
Prediction, not knowing leads to confident wrong answers (hallucinations).
Training data can be bad or poisoned, which can lead to bad behavior.
Fine-tuning is where safety rules are added, and attackers try to get around them (jailbreaks).
Context window is where prompt injection attacks can hide.
