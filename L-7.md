Lesson 7: Project Planning and Delegation

Course: AI Fluency: Framework & Foundations (Claude Academy) Section: Delegation Type: Written lesson (no video), a 4-step planning activity

Note: the project ideas and sample prompts below are my own suggestions, tailored to my goals. The lesson only lists project types.

Learning goal

Apply Delegation to a real, multi-step project that I will keep using for the rest of the course (Description, Discernment, Diligence).

1. The four steps in the lesson
Step	What to do
1. Choose a project	Substantial (several kinds of tasks), doable in about 1 hour, and something I'm genuinely interested in
2. Define vision and goals	Tell Claude my idea and ask it to question me until I know what success looks like and why it matters to me
3. Break down and delegate	List the major tasks. For each: what skills it needs, which parts are human strengths, which AI can handle, where collaboration helps most. Have a real back-and-forth, challenge assumptions
4. Create and save the plan	Write the tasks plus my delegation decisions. Save it (I return to it in later lessons)
2. Project types in the lesson
Communication: presentation, post/series for a general audience, proposal or pitch, professional bio with supporting materials
Research: summary of an emerging technology, dataset analysis, comparison of products or approaches, presentation of a historical event
Creative: short story outline with characters and plot, simple website structure, concept for a product or experience
Learning: structured learning plan, resource collection, tutorial or guide, study materials
3. One project idea per type (for me)
A. Communication: "My career-switch story"

Project: A professional bio for moving from inside sales (resume-writing services, 4 years) into AI security, plus a LinkedIn "About" section and a short message to send to people in the field. Why it fits: Uses my real experience; useful immediately. Pieces of work:

Task	Mostly	Why
Decide my story and what I want to be known for	Me	Only I know my goals
Draft versions of the bio	AI	Fast first drafts
Pick the version that sounds like me	Me	Judgment and voice
Check every claim is true	Me	AI can invent details

Try with Claude:

"I'm moving from 4 years in inside sales (resume-writing services) to AI security. Ask me questions one at a time to understand my story, strengths, and goal. Stop when you have enough to draft a bio."
"Break this project into tasks. For each, tell me what needs my judgment and what you can do."
"Draft three bios in different tones: confident, warm, concise."
"Now list anything in these drafts I should double-check for accuracy."
B. Research: "Map of entry-level paths into AI security"

Project: A short comparison of beginner-friendly roles, skills, and free learning resources for getting into AI security. Why it fits: Directly supports my career goal. Pieces of work:

Task	Mostly	Why
Decide what I want to know and compare	Me	My goals, my questions
Search and gather sources	AI (with web search)	Fast at collecting
Verify claims against the original sources	Me	Hallucination risk
Build the comparison table	AI	Good at structuring
Decide which path fits me	Me	Personal judgment

Try with Claude:

"I want to compare entry-level paths into AI security. Ask me questions about my background and what I want from the comparison."
"Use web search to find current information and list the source links for each claim."
"Put this into a table: role, skills needed, free ways to learn."
"Which of these claims are you least sure about?" (then I check them myself)
C. Creative: "The assistant that got tricked"

Project: A short story outline with characters and a plot where a helpful AI assistant is fooled by a hidden instruction. A fun way to understand prompt injection. Why it fits: Makes a security idea easy to remember. Pieces of work:

Task	Mostly	Why
Decide the story's idea and message	Me	The creative vision
Brainstorm characters and plot twists	Together	AI is good at many options
Choose what to keep, change, or drop	Me	Taste and judgment
Make sure the security idea is shown accurately	Me	Needs my understanding

Try with Claude:

"I want a short story about an AI assistant tricked by a hidden instruction in a document. Ask me questions about tone, setting, and message."
"Give me 5 character ideas and 3 plot directions."
"I'll pick what I like. Help me turn it into an outline."
D. Learning: "30-day AI security starter plan"

Project: A structured learning plan from zero to AI security basics, with simple study materials (flashcards, quizzes, a glossary). Why it fits: Keeps my learning organized and ties to my GitHub notes. Pieces of work:

Task	Mostly	Why
Decide how much time I have and what I want to reach	Me	My life, my pace
Draft the weekly plan	AI	Good at structure
Pick resources and check they're real and current	Me	AI may suggest wrong or outdated ones
Make flashcards and quizzes from my notes	AI	Quick to produce
Review and adjust weekly	Me	Accountability

Try with Claude:

"I'm a complete beginner aiming for AI security. I have about 1 hour a day. Ask me questions, then help me define what I should know in 30 days."
"Break this into weekly tasks and tell me which parts I should do myself."
"Turn my notes below into 10 flashcards and a 5-question quiz." (paste notes)
4. How to run any project on Claude (works for all four)
Start with the goal. Say what the project is and ask Claude to interview me one question at a time.
Define success. Write one sentence: "This is done when ___."
Break into tasks. Ask Claude to list the tasks, then take them one by one.
Decide per task. Ask: "What needs my judgment? What can you do? Where should we collaborate?"
Challenge it. Ask "What am I missing?" and "What could go wrong?"
Write the plan. A table with columns: Task / Who does it / Why.
Save it. In my GitHub notes and in my Claude Project, so I can reuse it in Description, Discernment, and Diligence.
Plan template
Task	Who does it (Me / AI / Together)	Why	How I'll check it

What extra information or skills would help me delegate better?
