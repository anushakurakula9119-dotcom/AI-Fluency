##Lesson 9: **Effective Prompting Techniques**

Course: AI Fluency: Framework & Foundations (Claude Academy) Section: Deep Dive 2: Effective prompting techniques

Note: the lesson page gives a short summary of the six techniques but no worked examples and no transcript. The examples and analogies below are my own, in simple words.

**Learning goals**
Explain what prompt engineering is and why it supports productive AI collaboration.
Apply six foundational prompting techniques.
Identify common patterns that lead to successful AI interactions.
Troubleshoot and refine prompts when responses miss the mark.
1. What is prompt engineering?

**Prompt engineering** = designing effective instructions for AI systems.

It combines ordinary good communication with AI-specific considerations.
It is the practical skill behind the Description competency from the last lesson.

Analogy: **giving directions to a new taxi driver**. The clearer the directions, the faster you arrive.

2. **The six foundational techniques**
#	Technique                       	                 Meaning (lesson)	                                                 Simple example (my own)
1	**Give context**	                    Be specific about what you want and why; include relevant background	      "I'm a sales rep writing to a customer who asked about pricing last week..."
2	**Show examples**	                         Demonstrate the output style or format you want	                     "Here is an email I liked. Write one in this style."
3	**Specify constraints**	                         Define format, length, and other requirements	                           "Under 100 words, 3 bullet points, no jargon."
4	**Break complex tasks into steps**	               Guide the AI through multi-step reasoning                   	"First list the objections, then rank them, then draft replies."
5	**Ask the AI to think first**	               Give it room to work through its process before answering	        "Think through the options before giving your final answer."
6	**Define the AI's role or tone**	                  Specify how it should communicate	                              "Act as a friendly career coach. Keep the tone encouraging."

**Memory trick**: C-E-C-B-T-R: Context, Examples, Constraints, Break into steps, Think first, Role/tone.

3. **Troubleshooting and refining prompts**

When the answer misses the mark:

Ask the AI to improve your prompt. The lesson calls this the "secret weapon."

Try: "Here's my prompt. What's unclear? Rewrite it so you could do a better job."

Iterate. Prompting is a back-and-forth process. Expect to refine based on results. It can be collaborative with the AI.

Use common successful patterns:
**A clear task overview
Format specifications
Explicit constraints
Relevant background information**

4. **Before and after** (my own example)

**Weak prompt**: "Help me with a resume."

**Stronger prompt** using the six techniques:

"You are a friendly career coach (role/tone). I'm moving from 4 years of inside sales into AI security (context). First ask me three questions about my experience, then think through which achievements matter most (steps + think first). Write a 4-bullet resume summary, under 80 words, no buzzwords (constraints). Here's a summary I like for style: [example] (example)."

**Quick revision**
Prompt engineering = writing clear, effective instructions for AI.
Six techniques: context, examples, constraints, steps, think first, role/tone.
Secret weapon: ask the AI to help improve your prompt.
Iterate: first try is rarely perfect.

**Good patterns**: task overview, format, constraints, background.

**Link to AI Security** (my goal)
Understanding how prompts steer AI is the base for understanding prompt injection and jailbreaks, where attackers craft instructions to override intended behavior.
Constraints and role definitions are the same ideas defenders use to keep AI within safe boundaries.
Iterating and testing prompts is the same habit used in red teaming (trying to break an AI system on purpose to find weaknesses).
