**Lesson 8: A Closer Look at Description**

Course: AI Fluency: Framework & Foundations (Claude Academy) 

Section: **Description** (second of the 4Ds) Lesson length: 15 min (video 4 min, plus exercise)

Note: the lesson page gives a short video summary (no transcript, no worked examples beyond the exercise). The analogies and examples below are my own, in simple words.

**Learning goals**
Understand how to communicate intentions to AI systems effectively.
Recognize why clear, purposeful communication matters.
Build skills in the three types of Description: **Product, Process, Performance**.

1. **What is Description?**

**Description** = communicating with AI in a way that creates a productive, collaborative environment.

**It is more than writing a prompt. It's about setting up how you and the AI will work together.
AI can't read your mind. The quality of the output often depends on how clearly you state what you need, how you want it done, and how you want the AI to interact with you.**

**Analogy**: asking a friend for help with homework. "Help me" gets a vague reply. "Explain photosynthesis in simple words, step by step, and quiz me at the end" gets exactly what you need.

2. **The three types of Description**
Type	                                 Meaning	                                                            The question it answers	                               Example
**Product Description**   	     Define what you want created: output, format, audience, style	                   "What should the result look like?"	"A 100-word friendly follow-up email for a customer who asked about pricing"
**Process Description**	       Guide how the AI should approach the task (can matter as much as the end goal)	   "How should you go about it?"	"First ask me 3 questions, then give two draft options, then explain which is stronger"
**Performance Description**    	Define how the AI should behave during the collaboration	                         "How should you act while working with me?"	"Be concise. Challenge my ideas instead of just agreeing. Tell me when you're unsure."

Memory trick: **Product = WHAT, Process = HOW, Performance = BEHAVIOR.**

**Everyday comparison** (ordering food):

Product: "A veg pizza, medium size."
Process: "Bake it thin and crispy, add the cheese last."
Performance: "Please call me if an ingredient is unavailable instead of substituting."

3. **Key points**
AI systems are interactive partners, not databases or vending machines.
Clear communication up front saves time and improves results.
Process description can matter as much as the end goal.
Performance description covers behavior: concise vs. detailed, challenging vs. supportive.
Description is part of a loop with Discernment (checking results), which comes later in the course.


5. **Exercise**: Bad Prompt Makeover
Ask Claude to give me some poorly written prompts to improve.
Rewrite each using Description thinking:
**Product: what exactly I want
Process: how I want Claude to approach it
Performance: how I want Claude to behave**

Discuss before and after with Claude. Ask how my improved version helps it respond better.
After about 5 minutes, switch roles: I give Claude poorly written prompts to fix. Notice what information Claude adds and how it organizes it.
Sample makeover (my own example)

**Before**: "Write an email to a customer."

**After**:

Product: "Write a 100-word follow-up email to a customer who asked about our resume-writing package pricing. Friendly tone, ends with an invitation to book a 15-minute call."
Process: "Ask me for any missing details first, then give two versions."
Performance: "Be concise. Point out anything in my details that seems unclear or risky to promise."
Prompt to start the exercise with Claude

"Give me 5 poorly written prompts (like something a beginner would type). I'll rewrite each one using Product, Process, and Performance, and then you tell me what I missed."

**Quick revision**
Description = communicating with AI to create a productive partnership.
Product = what I want. Process = how to approach it. Performance = how the AI should behave.
AI is an interactive partner, not a vending machine.
Clarity up front saves time.
Practice: Bad Prompt Makeover (fix bad prompts, then swap roles).

**Link to AI Security** (my goal)
Clear instructions are the first line of defense: vague instructions leave room for unsafe or unintended behavior.
Performance description (for example, "tell me when you're unsure," "don't assume") reduces overconfident or invented answers.
Understanding how instructions steer AI is the foundation for understanding prompt injection, where an attacker sneaks in instructions the AI wasn't meant to follow.
